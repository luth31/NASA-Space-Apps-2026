# Three-minute pitch — PyroBridge

Working live slot, not a verified Craiova rule. English; seven-slide order. Target 3:00 including brief visual pauses; rehearse at roughly 125–135 spoken words per minute. Say “satellite detections,” never “individual fires.” PyroBridge is provisional.

## 0:00–0:25 · Slide 1 · The user and problem

A fire manager looking back at a difficult season needs to know where activity concentrated and how it compares with the past. But a longer satellite record is not automatically a fairer comparison.

PyroBridge helps people read that record: first across geography, then through time, while keeping the limits of the evidence visible.

## 0:25–0:50 · Slide 2 · Why the instruments matter

NASA's MODIS and VIIRS instruments observe heat differently. They have different pixel sizes, sensitivities and opportunities to see the ground. A change in their raw counts can reflect the instrument as well as the landscape.

We use NASA FIRMS observations, preserve each sensor's identity, and ask what can be compared responsibly.

## 0:50–1:15 · Slide 3 · Explore

Explore makes the geography readable. The globe opens into an atlas, then a place leads to a study area and individual observations. You can inspect the actual acquisition time and sensor, or compare the two satellite records over the same scene.

The newer global snapshot stays separate from the historical studies.

## 1:15–1:45 · Slide 4 · A real finding

Here is a concrete discovery. In our two Greek study rectangles, August contains eighty-nine percent of the 2023 VIIRS detections: 4,178 out of 4,685.

Select the peak to move from month to day and acquisition hour. Across years reveals recurring timing; Two satellites shows measurement differences. These are descriptive patterns in the bundled observations, not fire-risk predictions or national totals.

## 1:45–2:20 · Slide 5 · What we contributed

Our contribution joins this investigation experience with a reproducible harmonization pipeline. It matches satellite passes, checks common usable coverage, retains observed zeros and tests candidate models on held-out regions.

The result is instructive: seasonal scaling beats our more complex models in the pilot. But that pilot contains only forty-eight eligible cell-pass examples across two regions. We report that scope instead of an impressive but misleading accuracy percentage.

## 2:20–2:45 · Slide 6 · What works and what comes next

Today, the app supports real observation exploration and historical pattern discovery. Harmonized monthly estimates remain locked.

The next milestone is independent monthly validation: complete observing opportunities, more regions and seasons, and a frozen evaluation protocol. Only supported results should become a historical comparison.

## 2:45–3:00 · Slide 7 · Closing

NASA makes the observations available. PyroBridge makes them easier to investigate, question and understand.

Choose a place. Follow a season. See what the satellites actually recorded.
