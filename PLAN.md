# PyroBridge product and validation plan

Planning notes for NASA Space Apps 2026. See [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md) for NASA data access, product versions, and imagery sources.

## Two project parts

1. **Model training and validation.** Prepare comparable MODIS and VIIRS observations, train the candidate approaches, run the geographic and time holdouts, and record estimates, coverage, uncertainty where validated, and benchmark results.
2. **Calendar and dashboard.** Let a fire manager explore reported hotspots globally, investigate an observation on the map, and examine a regional seasonal comparison where the model is supported. The calendar's primary measure is monthly VIIRS-equivalent hotspot counts. The globe, satellite comparison, and animated calendar reveal form the signature demo.

## Product direction

- **Audience and workflow.** A fire manager spots activity on a global map, opens a region, and checks how a selected month compares with the historical season. The map shows reported hotspot detections with their platform, acquisition time, and processing status. The calendar shows where historical comparisons have been validated.
- **Geographic scope.** Build for global investigations and precomputed historical summaries. Start the harmonized calendar with three feasibility cases: Evros for the flagship scene, Evia for an earlier overlap check, and Argentine Gran Chaco for the first check outside Greece. Choose training and holdout periods based on usable paired coverage. Expand to a geographically distributed set after measuring coverage, runtime, and transfer performance. [Copernicus Evros imagery and timing](https://atmosphere.copernicus.eu/august-wildfires-ravage-northern-central-greece) · [NASA Evia imagery](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/fire-consumes-large-swaths-of-greece-148682/) · [NASA Gran Chaco imagery](https://modis.gsfc.nasa.gov/gallery/individual.php?db_date=2020-05-12)
- **Recency.** Aim for the latest complete month supported by standard processing and validation. FIRMS replaces recent near-real-time records with standard data as it becomes available, with an archive lag of about five months. Show the actual month through which the calendar is comparable. Recent global detections can use NOAA-20 or NOAA-21 when available. Each platform needs its own checked transfer before its detections enter the historical comparison. Suomi NPP's FIRMS record ends November 2, 2026. [FIRMS archive](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/download/) · [FIRMS data availability](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/api/data_availability/)
- **Approved demo sequence.** Start with a full-screen globe and use a sweeping camera descent into the actual dated satellite scene. Lock the camera while Aqua and VIIRS detections reveal in sync, then carry the selected date and region into the expanding calendar. Animate the raw-to-harmonized reveal with stable axes, color scale, and region. End on the seasonal comparison and measured validation result. Make direct exploration responsive and provide reduced motion through the browser preference. [NASA Worldview](https://worldview.earthdata.nasa.gov/) · [W3C interaction animation guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)

## Dashboard and demo

- **Globe.** Open on the latest timestamped observation snapshot and offer the featured Greece case in one click. Represent dense areas with clusters at globe scale and individual observation markers as the user zooms in. Add a list for dense selections, with location, acquisition time, and sensor in the preview. In sparse views, use a small number of imagery cards when suitable dated scenes are available. A leader line connects each card to the reported pixel center. Show the imagery date separately when it differs from the detection date.
- **Investigation.** Any selected detection flies the camera to its reported point and opens its dossier with coordinates, timestamp, satellite, confidence, processing status, measured FRP in MW where available, and nearby observations. Include dated imagery, time playback, and optional FRP columns as data and polish time allow. Open the smallest precomputed area that supports a historical comparison. Outline that area, show the selected month's count, historical median, and uncertainty, and let the user widen the area while keeping the observation pinned. Selecting a month updates the map and investigation together. Make the validation and data dates accessible from the dashboard.
- **Event context and exports.** Prefetch related official reports and source links from curated NASA EONET events and Copernicus EMS activations where time and geometry support an association. Link to original coverage and attribute it. Display possible associations as such. Evros has an official Copernicus activation for the featured case. Where an investigation has a supported historical calendar, offer CSV for observations and calendar values and a PDF investigation brief with the map, dates, sources, coverage, and uncertainty. Generate both from the published data the dashboard displays. These exports serve day-to-day investigations. The recorded submission and live pitch have their own presentation flow. [NASA EONET API](https://eonet.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/v3) · [Copernicus EMS API](https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/about/how-to-harvest-cems-mapping-data/) · [Evros activation](https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/activations/EMSR686/)
- **Presentation.** The guided demo uses the same scene selection, camera moves, sensor comparison, and calendar controls as ordinary exploration. A visible Play demo control starts the sequence. Pause, resume, and replay let judges explore at their own pace. Record real interactions and edit the global submission cut to 30 seconds. Use the team's 3–5 minute in-person slot for live exploration, the longer sensor comparison, and validation proof.
- **Training handoff.** The dashboard loads versioned, precomputed tiles and investigation data with monthly estimates, original counts, coverage flags, uncertainty where validated, and the held-out scorecard. Publish new observation snapshots in batches. A click reads published results. Keep the data and dated imagery needed for the stage sequence available locally.

## Global depth and compute budget

**Initial measurement.** NASA's public sample contains 74,605 global S-NPP observations from most of one day, July 12, 2023. On the workspace's 11-core Apple M3 Pro with 36 GiB RAM, an in-memory Python spot check transferred 6,077,404 bytes in 2.0 s. CSV parsing took 0.132 s and counting occupied cells took 0.011–0.013 s per resolution, median of five runs. At 0.25° there were 7,412 occupied cells. At 0.05° there were 21,801. This measures point ingestion and binning only. It uses a near-real-time sample and measures no archive backfill, coverage joins, training, serving, or browser performance. [NASA FIRMS sample](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/content/academy/data_ingest/firms_data_ingest.html)

| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1° | 111 km | 19.4 million |
| 0.5° | 56 km | 77.8 million |
| 0.25° | 28 km | 311 million |
| 0.05° | 5.6 km | 7.78 billion |

These counts include ocean and all months. Longitude width shrinks toward the poles. Use chunked storage and explicit coverage status so omitted data remains distinguishable from observed zeros. Start the calendar feasibility benchmark at 0.25° and 0.5°. Test 0.05° locally and expose individual detection locations independently at closer zoom. Benchmark model support at each calendar scale we publish. NASA's monthly 0.25° MODIS CMG has fire counts, cloud fraction, and sample counts, making it a candidate for cross-checking MODIS coverage. FIRMS gridded display counts lack a paired coverage denominator, and VIIRS `VNP14A1.002` uses 750 m detections. The chosen 375 m reference still requires a checked source of coverage. On one NASA archive day, 204 listed S-NPP geolocation granules alone total about 30.7 GB from their listed file sizes. [MODIS product guide](https://modis-land.gsfc.nasa.gov/pdf/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) · [VIIRS daily gridded product](https://data.nasa.gov/dataset/viirs-npp-thermal-anomalies-and-fire-daily-l3-global-1km-sin-grid-v002-af393) · [NASA LAADS July 1, 2024 listing](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/archive/allData/5200/VNP03IMG/2024/183/)

**CPU-first batch processing.** Stream point lists into date and spatial partitions with DuckDB and Parquet, then precompute map tile counts, monthly summaries, and supported predictions. Use archive metadata to request matching swaths for geographically stratified region and season blocks, with a mix of fire regimes, latitudes, cloud conditions, and low-activity periods. Validate on withheld geography and time. A stratified sample can train and test transfer, while publishing a historical estimate still requires the affected area to have supported inputs and coverage. Training is required. Fit LightGBM with the Poisson objective and the negative-binomial comparison on the prepared aggregate feature table. Scikit-learn's histogram gradient boosting with Poisson loss is an alternate implementation of the same model family if the local LightGBM build slows setup. Evaluate a GPU only if measured fit time takes a material share of the batch budget. LightGBM supports CPU on macOS, and its documented GPU builds target other systems. [DuckDB Parquet](https://duckdb.org/docs/stable/data/parquet/overview) · [NASA CMR spatial and temporal filters](https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/site/docs/search/api.html) · [LightGBM count objective](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/stable/Parameters.html#objective) · [LightGBM installation](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/stable/Installation-Guide.html) · [Scikit-learn histogram gradient boosting](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.HistGradientBoostingRegressor.html)

**Case-study timing demo.** Six public FIRMS yearly CSVs for Greece (2021 and 2023) and Argentina (2020) totaled **48.1 MB and 599,177 detections**. On the M3 Pro, the files fetched in **6.4 seconds** using four concurrent downloads. DuckDB grouped daytime Aqua and S-NPP detections into 0.25° cells by week and joined cells with detections from both streams in **0.04–0.13 seconds per country-year**, median of five runs. LightGBM 4.7.0 fitted a 100-round Poisson model in **0.095 seconds** and predicted the held-out subset in **0.005 seconds**, medians across five runs per country holdout on 15,464 shared positive grid-weeks, using four CPU threads. The older MODIS CSVs report version `6.03`, and Greece 2023 reports `61.03`. Use a pinned, consistent collection for the scientific run. This demo measures model mechanics and runtime on point data. Filtered grid-weeks omit observed zeros and contain no swath coverage checks. No scientific performance score was computed. [NASA FIRMS archive](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/download/) · [Public yearly CSV index](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/data/country/yearly_summary_files.txt)

**Paired science input.** NASA CMR lists a candidate Evros pair at 11:45 UTC (Aqua) and 11:48 UTC (S-NPP) on August 22, 2023. The two fire masks and two geolocation files total **230.8 MB** from their listed file sizes. The catalog and sizes are public. Reading the files requires Earthdata Login. The nominal granule starts are three minutes apart. Check acquisition timing over the actual area and usable common coverage before including the pair. For the initial check, use this pair and one quieter candidate pass. Expand to earlier Greece observations and a geographically separate site after measuring that step. The Argentina point timing establishes an ingestion candidate only. [NASA CMR search docs](https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/site/docs/search/api.html) · [Earthdata Login](https://urs.earthdata.nasa.gov/)

**First batch budget.** Target two hours end to end on available CPU for a publishable case-study bundle, including data transfer, coverage matching, mandatory training, validation, and publishing. Measure transfer bytes, peak RAM, disk use, fit and inference time, output size, and click-to-detail latency. Check the actual coverage of the initial swath pairs before extending their dates or geography. Expand if the measured data volume, runtime, and validation support it. Consider a GPU if training becomes a material part of the budget. Maintain global point investigations as calendar coverage expands.

## What the model estimates

The primary quantity is a monthly **VIIRS-equivalent hotspot count under comparable observing conditions**. The estimate applies to a supported region and time period. A VIIRS figure counts detected pixels, which may include several pixels for one event. Historical map points show the original detections. Show uncertainty where it can be validated. If both models lack support, show the available original observations and mark the historical comparison unavailable. Treat July 2002 as the start of the Aqua record and any earlier Terra-only segment as a separate transfer problem. [Aqua MODIS product](https://doi.org/10.5067/MODIS/MYD14.061) · [NASA VIIRS active-fire products](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/Products/NASA/FireESDR.html)

Start with daytime calibration pairs from standard Aqua MODIS and S-NPP VIIRS data. Match on actual pass times and common usable land coverage, using Level-2 fire masks and geolocation to distinguish observed zeros from unknowns. Check match yield across the drift period beginning after December 2021. Record valid area and pass opportunities before comparing monthly aggregates. Validate nighttime separately if enough paired observations are available. Use features available from historical MODIS and time-appropriate ancillary data, such as MODIS counts, available FRP summaries, valid observed area, viewing geometry and season. VIIRS counts and masks help construct and assess calibration targets. Run the calendar estimation path on held-out MODIS inputs with VIIRS withheld until scoring, so performance reflects what the historical app can calculate. Report pairs retained after coverage matching. [NASA Aqua mission](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/aqua/) · [Matched Aqua and VIIRS study](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/10/1561) · [MODIS fire product guide](https://modis-land.gsfc.nasa.gov/pdf/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf)

**Monthly comparison.** Define the reference daytime observation effort before producing a calendar. Specify how usable area and pass opportunities contribute to each monthly count, and validate the conversion from matched passes to the published monthly measure. If that conversion lacks support, publish only comparisons for months with comparable observed coverage. Compute the historical median for the same calendar month and analysis area using a documented set of eligible historical years. Exclude the selected year and show the number of contributing years. Label observed counts, modeled estimates, and unavailable comparisons in the app and exports.

## Candidate harmonization approaches

| Priority | Approach | What it tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main candidate | Gradient-boosted count regression, such as LightGBM with a Poisson objective | Whether nonlinear effects of MODIS activity, viewing geometry, and season improve held-out VIIRS count estimates. |
| Fallback candidate | Regularized negative-binomial regression with an exposure offset where valid coverage supports it | Whether a simpler count model transfers more consistently. The exposure term accounts for usable observation effort, and the model allows count variability beyond Poisson variance. |
| Baseline | Seasonal scaling with each season's ratio shrunk toward a pooled ratio on comparable coverage | Establishes how much either model adds beyond a transparent correction. It maps MODIS zero to zero and provides no independent evidence about small events missed by MODIS. |
| Experiment | Two-part count model, estimating a positive count's probability and size separately | Worth testing if observed zeros and positive counts behave differently, with enough examples to evaluate both parts. |
| Experiment | Common-grid active-cell days | Both sensors report whether a coarser cell had at least one hotspot on a comparable observation day. This tests whether a lower-resolution activity measure transfers better. It has its own validation and unit. |

Run the first three before investing in experiments. Test unchanged MODIS counts against VIIRS as an additional control. A candidate earns its role from held-out results. Where the main model is outside training support, use the fallback only if its own validation supports that case. If coverage or transfer evidence is insufficient, show original observations and flag the comparison as unavailable. Apply the same policy to the first Terra years and any future satellite handoff. [LightGBM objectives](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/stable/Parameters.html#objective) · [Negative-binomial counts and offsets](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/aod/html/negbin.html)

## Validation and reporting

1. **Hold out entire regions.** Define non-overlapping geographic regions before fitting. In each fold, withhold the region's full overlap record and train on the others. Use the same eligible held-out rows for comparisons. Keep adjacent cells and observations from the same fire episode together. Fit learned preprocessing, scaling, and tuning inside training folds. Show how much usable coverage each fold has and where new conditions fall outside training support.
2. **Hold out later time.** Lock a continuous block of later overlap years or seasons before model selection. The blueprint's 2022–2025 window is a candidate, subject to enough common coverage after Aqua's orbit drift. Use the earlier data for candidate and threshold selection, with geographic folds for transfer. Evaluate the chosen pipeline once on the locked block. If enough matched data exist, additionally test a later period in a wholly unseen region. If few regions qualify, report within-region time validation and name the regions it covers.
3. **Report absolute error, bias, and supported coverage.** Give mean absolute error (MAE) in hotspots per region-month, signed bias, the unweighted mean and range of regional MAEs, and counts of requested, data-eligible, and estimated periods. Include errors for high-activity months. Report interval coverage alongside interval width if the app displays prediction intervals. For a headline percentage, use `100 × (1 − model MAE / baseline MAE)` on the same held-out rows when the baseline MAE is positive. Percentage error per month breaks on observed zero months. If the calendar identifies unusually active months, evaluate that decision separately against observed VIIRS. [Roberts et al. on blocked validation](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.02881) · [Cawley and Talbot on model selection](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html) · [Hyndman and Koehler on error measures](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2006.03.001)
4. **Refit after freezing the results.** Once the model, fallback rules, and reporting are locked, the deployment model can be refit using all eligible overlap. Keep the benchmark predictions and settings so we can reproduce the quoted result. Describe the number as held-out performance of the method on the tested overlap, with region and date scope. For example, the eventual claim can be “X% lower mean absolute error than seasonal scaling on held-out region-months,” accompanied by the measured absolute error. Pre-2012 estimates have no contemporaneous VIIRS reference, so their reliability depends on similarity to the validated conditions.

**Release decision.** After the coverage pilot, agree on minimum usable coverage, how many matched periods and historical years support a comparison, and the error and bias tolerances for the model. Freeze those criteria before scoring the final holdout. Require a meaningful improvement over seasonal scaling for the complex model to ship. The simpler model can ship where it meets its own accuracy and coverage criteria. If neither qualifies, the app can still show a dated sensor comparison and label the historical estimate unavailable. Call out the validated dates and regions alongside any reported result.
