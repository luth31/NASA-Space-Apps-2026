## Get the active-fire observations

1. **Historical regional export.** Open FIRMS Archive Download, sign in with a free Earthdata account or email verification code, choose CSV, select MODIS and the relevant VIIRS platform(s), then set a date range and draw a custom box or polygon. The service emails a link after processing. It covers records older than the last seven days and replaces recent near-real-time (NRT) records with standard science-quality records after approximately five months. Record the cutoff of standard data for each platform before choosing the analysis end date. [FIRMS Archive Download](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/download/) · [NASA FIRMS training, Part 3](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/tutorials/) · [Earthdata Login](https://urs.earthdata.nasa.gov/)
2. **Small standard subset and provenance check.** For a short candidate window, FIRMS Area API accepts an area as west, south, east, north and one to five days per request. It requires a free `MAP_KEY`. Use `MODIS_SP`, `VIIRS_SNPP_SP`, `VIIRS_NOAA20_SP`, or `VIIRS_NOAA21_SP` for standard processing. Use the Data Availability endpoint to check standard and NRT ranges and the Missing Data page for known outages. Use the archive export for the multi-year region and check its processing labels against availability. [Area API](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/api/area/) · [Data Availability](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/api/data_availability/) · [Missing Data](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/api/missing_data/)
3. **Product and platform IDs.** FIRMS advertises the coverage below, which includes recent NRT data where available. `MCD14ML` and `Vxx14IMGML` are monthly lists of individual point detections. L2 products are swaths. VIIRS names ending in `_NRT` such as `VNP14IMGTDL_NRT` identify a separate processing stream. New Suomi NPP delivery ended November 2, 2026. NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 provide continuing VIIRS observations. Treat extension to either platform as a distinct calibration check. ## Evidence for harmonization

- **Match what the sensors actually observed.** Aqua and Suomi NPP are a candidate afternoon pair. Use actual UTC acquisition timing, shared swath footprint, comparable day/night observations, and usable common coverage. NASA documents Aqua orbit drift after December 2021, so check time-of-day differences on the proposed 2022–2025 holdout. Terra’s different sampling makes applying the Aqua fit to the Terra-only early record an additional transfer claim. One published study used coincident swath subsets within ±5 minutes for a particular FRP analysis. The region, timing tolerance and supported sample size need their own check. [Aqua mission](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/aqua/) · [Schroeder and colleagues on the VIIRS product](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) · [Matched-overpass FRP study](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/10/1561)
- **Count the opportunity to observe.** With a free Earthdata login, use Earthdata Search or LAADS to find the L2 granules for the area and dates. Use the fire masks and QA to distinguish valid observed land with no detected fire from cloud, unprocessed pixels, or absent coverage. Pair with the matching geolocation granules (`MYD03` in the corresponding collection and `VNP03IMG` for Suomi NPP) for full coverage and viewing geometry. FIRMS CSVs describe detections only. The MODIS guide flags potentially severe bias from aggregating its point lists for periods shorter than about eight days and recommends considering the gridded fire products. [Earthdata Search](https://search.earthdata.nasa.gov/) · [Aqua MODIS L2 record](https://doi.org/10.5067/MODIS/MYD14.061) · [Suomi NPP L2 archive](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/products/VNP14IMG) · [Aqua geolocation](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/products/MYD03) · [Suomi NPP geolocation](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/products/VNP03IMG) · [MODIS guide, §7.1.1](https://modis-land.gsfc.nasa.gov/pdf/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf)
- **Compare against simple controls.** Candidate tests are the unchanged sensor series and a constant or seasonal scaling learned on earlier overlap. Compare with any proposed model on identical later overlap and common coverage, and hold out an additional region before claiming geographic transfer. Report signed bias, an absolute error measure, matched sample size, and the result by season or observation geometry when support allows. Keep sensor, collection, day/night and original confidence throughout. MODIS confidence is numeric and VIIRS confidence is categorical. If filtering by the VIIRS monthly `Type` field, check the archive revision given NASA’s correction for misclassified static sources. FRP in MW measures radiant power at observation time. FRP ratios can vary with geometry and detectability. The VIIRS 375 m algorithm retrieves FRP from co-located 750 m radiance and can split it among multiple detected pixels. The 2012 discontinuity, change in error, and benefit of a complex model are all results to measure. [VIIRS C2 guide](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/archive/Document%20Archive/Science%20Data%20Product%20Documentation/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) · [FIRMS archive correction](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/web-services/) · [FRP intercomparison](https://doi.org/10.1029/2017JD027823) · [Coverage-aware FRP fusion example](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/18/10/1650)

## Real imagery and candidate scenes

- **Date-matched regional imagery.** NASA Worldview can compare dated MODIS and VIIRS true-color imagery and thermal anomalies. GIBS documents the underlying tile service. NASA Worldview Snapshots can export a bounded dated scene for a local presentation. Keep scene, sensor, UTC date, layer and source credit with the asset. Live tiles need a network connection. [Worldview](https://worldview.earthdata.nasa.gov/) · [GIBS access](https://nasa-gibs.github.io/gibs-api-docs/access-basics/) · [Snapshot export](https://wvs.earthdata.nasa.gov/) · [NASA media usage](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)
- **Detailed scene and offline basemap.** Copernicus Browser offers dated Sentinel-2 optical scenes, with 10 m visible bands, for land context or clear-sky post-fire imagery. Sign in to export imagery and use the required Copernicus credit for public use, including the modified-data wording when applicable. Downloadable Natural Earth shaded relief and boundaries are public domain and provide a reproducible offline regional basemap. The thermal product determines detection precision at every imagery zoom level. [Copernicus Browser](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/Applications/Browser.html) · [Sentinel-2](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/data-collections/copernicus-sentinel-missions/sentinel-2) · [Sentinel data attribution](https://ads.atmosphere.copernicus.eu/licences/ec-sentinel) · [Natural Earth shaded relief](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/50m-raster-data/50m-natural-earth-1/) · [Natural Earth terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/)
- **Candidate regions.** Greece offers a documented August 2021 Evia scene from NASA Earth Observatory and an August 2023 Evros example with Copernicus atmospheric imagery and mapped burn extent. Gran Chaco offers a CONAE-relevant comparison and a NASA Aqua true-color acquisition from May 10, 2020 with visible smoke. Evros falls within the proposed 2022–2025 held-out period. Check paired pass coverage and select comparable quiet periods before using any of these scenes as a data benchmark. [NASA Evia scene](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/fire-consumes-large-swaths-of-greece-148682/) · [Copernicus Evros event](https://atmosphere.copernicus.eu/august-wildfires-ravage-northern-central-greece) · [Evros post-event mapping](https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/activations/EMSN166/FIREDELINEATION/) · [NASA Gran Chaco scene](https://modis.gsfc.nasa.gov/gallery/individual.php?db_date=2020-05-12)
- **Demo labels.** Use **observed hotspot detections**, **estimated reference-sensor activity**, and **insufficient observation** where applicable. Include the area, date range, units, platform, processing mode, and imagery timestamp. A hotspot marks a thermal anomaly within a satellite footprint and can reflect one portion of a larger event or a non-fire heat source. Show any footprint approximation as such. Keep contextual burn extent, model estimates and current NRT data identifiable in charts, legends, and exports. ## Pre-development checklist

1. Choose one initial area, one user decision the calendar should support, and the quantity or reference-sensor scale that will appear in it. Record the area boundary and candidate demo window.
2. Confirm archive login, standard-processing date ranges, and the ability to obtain matched L2 fire masks and geolocation for a busy window plus a quiet window. Define what qualifies as usable coverage and how incomplete periods appear.
3. Record the proposed holdout, simple controls, error measures, and the claims that each experiment could support. Decide whether the Terra-only years and recent NOAA-20/21 NRT are in scope based on evidence for those transfers.
4. Verify imagery dates, local export availability, and attribution for the selected area. Confirm the local presentation rules and prepare the global demo to the published length limit.
